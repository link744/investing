The split

Nominal 5.28% locks a coupon. Real 2.92% locks inflation protection. The 13-week T-bill locks no rate risk at 4.1%.

The ask

You don't pick a bond to beat 4.1% forever. You pick it to lock a yield — and the real yield you lock in today is the highest since 2023.

What you lock in by buying now

Yesterday's post answered why a 5.28% 10-year is the first 10-year bond at this nominal rate since 2007, and what a rate cut does to it. This post answers what you lock in by buying now — because a bond's yield is the rate you're paid forever, and the difference between a 10-year bond and a 13-week T-bill is how long you lock it in.

The 10-year nominal (5.28%) is at a 2007 high. The 10-year real (2.92%) is at a 2023 high — and it's 115 basis points above what the real yield was at the 2007 nominal peak (1.77%). That's the number that matters: if the Fed cuts 50bp this year, your locked-in nominal drops to ~4.8%. Your real yield — the part that beats inflation — is still the highest it's been since October 2023.

Real yield is the 2023 high, not the 2007 high. The 10-year nominal was 5.26% in June 2007. The 10-year real was 1.77%. Today: nominal 5.28%, real 2.92%. The nominal is at a 2007 high. The real is at a 2023 high — and it's 115bp above the 2007 real peak. That's what you're buying: inflation protection at the highest level since 2023.
The 13-week T-bill is 4.10%. That's the floor — the rate you get with zero rate risk. Every other allocation is a trade: you give up rate safety for a higher nominal, or you give up nominal for inflation protection. The 13-week is the baseline; the 10-year bond is the premium you pay for locking in a yield.
Where the $100k money market goes. You don't put all $100k in the 10-year bond. You put a portion in a 13-week T-bill (4.10%, zero rate risk), a portion in a short Treasury fund (VBTLX, 4.45% NAV, 4-year duration), and a portion in a TIPS fund (VTIP, ~2.9% real). The TIPS fund is the one that locks in the 2.92% real yield — the highest since 2023.
The lock-in math. A 10-year bond at 5.28% means: if the Fed cuts 50bp this year, your bond is worth more (price up ~8%). If the Fed holds, your bond is worth face value. If rates rise 50bp, your bond is worth less (price down ~8%). The real yield you lock in — 2.92% — is the part that's insulated from the rate cut. That's the 2023 high.

So the answer to "is now a good time to buy Treasuries/TIPS for high rates" is: the 10-year nominal is at a 2007 high, and the 10-year real is at a 2023 high. If you're locking in a yield — not speculating on the next cut — now is the time. The 13-week T-bill is the floor; the 10-year bond is the premium.

And the answer to "where should a $100k money-market allocation go" is: split it. A portion in 13-week T-bills (4.10%, zero rate risk). A portion in a short Treasury fund (VBTLX, 4.45% NAV). A portion in a TIPS fund (VTIP, ~2.9% real). The TIPS fund locks in the 2.92% real yield — the highest since 2023. That's the allocation.
