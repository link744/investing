When Do the Next Auction Windows Open? 2-Year Note & TIPS, Dated

Short version: three windows matter in the next six weeks — 5-year TIPS on Thursday, Oct 22, 2-year note on Monday, Oct 26, and 10-year TIPS on Thursday, Nov 19 (all 2026). You can't bid early: the order only exists for the auction day, and your money moves on the issue date, not the bid date. Below is the calendar, both places to bid (TreasuryDirect and a Vanguard brokerage account — yes, both work), and the two gotchas.

What an auction actually is

The Treasury sells new Treasuries and TIPS at fixed dates, not continuously. On the auction day, everyone who wants that exact issue submits a bid; the Treasury sets one clearing yield, and every non-competitive bidder gets the security at that yield. If you want the specific 2-year note or 5-year TIPS — not "TIPS-ish" fund shares — you bid the auction. That's why "when does the next window open" has a real calendar answer.

The three upcoming windows. You bid on the auction date (dot) and the security settles into your account on the issue date (diamond) — a few days later, not the next day. 5-yr TIPS date is live-confirmed from the TreasuryDirect feed; the 2-yr note and 10-yr TIPS dates come from the Treasury's official tentative schedule (the 2-yr note gets its official announcement Oct 22 and lands in the live calendar then).

Two practical details. The auction day is the only day you can bid — there is no early-bird window, and you don't "reserve" a slot in advance. And money moves on the issue date: your bid on Oct 22 is paid when the security settles on Oct 30, so have the funds available then (more on settlement below).

Where to bid: TreasuryDirect vs a Vanguard brokerage account

Both work, and both are fee-free on the auction itself. The difference is minimums and where the security ends up.

Route 1 — TreasuryDirect (lowest minimum)

  1. Have a TreasuryDirect account (free) and a linked checking account.
  2. On auction day: Buy & Sell → Purchase Treasuries, pick the security (e.g., the 5-yr TIPS).
  3. Bid non-competitive — the only bid type for individuals on this site. You accept whatever yield the auction sets.
  4. Enter the amount: $100 minimum, then $100 steps (e.g., $2,500).
  5. Submit before the non-competitive close (11:00 AM ET for bills; notes/TIPS close times are stated in that auction's announcement — check it the day of).
  6. Result: security is deposited on the issue date; payment comes from your linked account that day. Bid results are visible after 5 PM ET on auction day.

Route 2 — Vanguard brokerage (the one people think doesn't exist)

  1. Log in to your brokerage account (the brokerage interface, not the mutual-fund/IRA-only view).
  2. Buy & Sell → Fixed Income → Treasuries → Auction — new-issue Treasuries and new-issue TIPS are orderable here. (The TIPS order screen is in the TIPS section, up for the day before/auction day.)
  3. Enter the amount: $1,000 minimum, $1,000 increments. Non-competitive — same auction yield as everyone else.
  4. No commission, no fee on the new-issue order (fees only apply if you later trade it in the secondary market). No auto-roll feature at Vanguard.
  5. Settles on the issue date into your account; hold to maturity or sell later.

Which one? TreasuryDirect if your position is small ($100 minimum, and it's the only place for I-Bonds later). Vanguard if you're already there and want the specific issue alongside the rest of your portfolio — or if you'd rather skip single issues entirely and buy a TIPS fund: VTP (total TIPS market, all maturities, the closest thing to a "Vanguard TIPS ETF") or VTIP (short-term TIPS, under 5 years). Funds trade any day at market price; they don't bid the auction for you.

Gotcha 1 — it settles on the issue date, not the day after you bid

You bid Oct 22; the money leaves and the security arrives on the issue date, Oct 30. The gap is a few days for these auctions (T-bill-style T+1 settlement is the secondary-market story — when you buy an existing Treasury mid-day at a broker, it settles one business day later). Plan the funding for the issue date, not the bid date, and don't count on the security existing until settlement. If your bank account is short on issue day, the order can fail after the auction is already over.

Gotcha 2 — non-competitive means you don't pick the yield; the market does

You get the clearing yield the auction sets — whatever that turns out to be on the day. If yields have risen by auction day (rates up), you actually get a better yield than the one quoted when you read this post; if yields have fallen, you get the lower one. There's no price slippage and no rejection of a non-competitive bid, but there's no locking a yield in advance either. That's the trade for skipping the competitive bid: certainty of filling, uncertainty of the exact rate. (Competitive bids — where you name your yield — exist, but they go through a bank/broker/dealer, cap out at 35% of the offering, and aren't available on TreasuryDirect.)

So, in plain English

  • Want the 5-yr TIPS? Thursday, Oct 22, 2026 — it's the next window, and it's already live in the TreasuryDirect calendar.
  • Want the 2-yr note? Monday, Oct 26, 2026. Four days after the TIPS; it's on the official tentative schedule and gets formally announced Oct 22.
  • Want the 10-yr TIPS? Thursday, Nov 19, 2026 — next month. 10-year TIPS run roughly monthly, so there will be another window soon after.
  • How: TreasuryDirect from $100, or a Vanguard brokerage account from $1,000 — both non-competitive, both $0 fee on the order. Have the cash ready for the issue date, and understand you're buying at whatever yield the auction sets that day.
Bottom line

The windows aren't mysterious — there's a calendar, and the next one opens Oct 22. The only real constraints are: bid on the day, fund it a few days later on the issue date, and accept the auction's clearing yield. If you'd rather have TIPS exposure without standing by the calendar, VTP or VTIP gets you there any trading day — but you own a basket, not a dated issue.

Honest caveats

Dates verified 2026-10-10 against the live TreasuryDirect upcoming-auctions JSON feed (5-yr TIPS: confirmed) and the Treasury's tentative 6-month auction schedule PDF (2-yr note, 10-yr TIPS). The 2-yr note and 10-yr TIPS dates can shift if Treasury moves the schedule — the 2-yr note's official announcement lands Oct 22, which is the confirmation date. Bid close times vary by auction and are stated in each auction's announcement. I did not find a public "VBTPX"-style Vanguard TIPS ETF under that exact ticker — Vanguard's TIPS ETFs are VTP (total) and VTIP (short-term); VAIPX/VIPSX is the actively managed mutual fund version, not an ETF. This is a how-to, not a yield forecast — the auction yield is whatever the auction sets.
