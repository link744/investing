Bond Recap Time

October 11, 2026 · all posts
The recap

The stock market has been on fire. But underneath the headlines, the bond market has been changing — and it's been changing for you, not against you. For the first time in a long time, bonds are paying real money to hold them. So we've been posting about this a lot. If you've been following along — or just woke up and want the thread — here's the recap: what's going on, and the two moves it points to.

Why now

The short version: the market is telling you bonds are worth buying again, and the math says the same thing. We walked through it in Is now a good time to buy US bonds? — that post is the “why”. This post is the “so what do I actually do.”

Move 1 — park your spare cash in short-term bonds

Money sitting in a checking account earning anything less than 4.62% is the easiest cash to move first. Two good homes for it:

  • Treasuries or TIPS. The difference is the expected vs. real interest rate — one pays you what's on the curve, the other pays you what's left after inflation. We drew out the curve you'd be buying: nominal vs. real, and that's the one to look at before picking.
  • How you actually buy them: you get in through the auction windows. We dated when the next auction windows open for the 2-year note and TIPS — that's your shopping list and your calendar.

Move 2 — shift some VOO into a total-bond index fund

This one is slower and more deliberate: moving part of your stock position into something like VBTLX (Vanguard Total Bond Market Index).

  • Don't time it. Start buying now, but not all in one go. If the Fed hikes, you'll see a short-term drop — that's normal, and we put a number on it in What happens to $100,000 in VBTLX if the Fed hikes the month you buy? The drop is why you don't do it all at once.
  • How much to move? That's a number, not a feeling. The Bond Tent Calculator gives you your bond % at every age — enter your age and your planned retirement age, and it does the rest.
  • When to move? Slowly. If you're 5–10 years from retirement, the answer is yes, sell some VOO and buy bonds — in several bite sized moves, not one shot.
  • What it looks like over time: here's what $100,000 in VBTLX grows into over 1, 5, and 10 years, in dollars. That's the whole argument for the shift, in plain numbers.
TL;DR: put your spare cash into short-term Treasuries (or TIPS, depending on the curve you want) — and start shifting your retirement portfolio into bonds slowly. The cash move is quick; the portfolio move is a series of small steps, not a one-time decision.
