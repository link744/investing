What $100,000 in VBTLX grows into — 1, 5, and 10 years, in dollars

October 10, 2026 · all posts
The plain-English read

Short version: put $100,000 into VBTLX today and hold, and on the base case it's about $105,500 in a year, $130,700 in five, and $170,800 in ten — the money roughly doubles in ~13 years. The honest band is wide at 1 year ($100.4k–$110.5k), tight at 5 ($123k–$132k), and wide again at 10 ($151.2k–$177k).

Why the shape is like that: it's the duration. VBTLX runs ~5.7 years of interest-rate sensitivity. Hold short of that and you're selling into price, so 1 year is a coin flip that depends on where rates land. Hold out to the duration and the price wobble washes out, so 5 years lands near 5.5% no matter what. Hold well past it and you've locked in that yield for a decade — which is exactly why the 10-year number is the most reliable of the three, and why the 1-year number is the one to treat as a range, not a forecast.

The one thing to keep in mind: every number here assumes a flat 5.5% curve and nominal, pre-tax dollars. If the Fed keeps rates higher, the base case holds up; if yields roll down, the 5-yr and 10-yr base is optimistic and the Low column is the truer one. But the structure — the tight 5-yr band and the reliable 10-yr earn — is a property of holding a bond fund out to its duration, and it's the defensible core of the whole projection.
